      text: FOCUS Report Preparation Starts With Reliable Books
      color: text-dark
      type: TitleBlock
    text: |
      A FOCUS filing is only as reliable as the financial records supporting it. The general ledger, trial balance, bank reconciliations, revenue, expenses, accruals, capital activity, and regulatory schedules should agree before the report is prepared or reviewed.

      My support can include a documented close process, review of underlying balances, preparation or review of the applicable FOCUS schedules, reconciliation to supporting workpapers, and coordination of the filing calendar. The objective is not simply to submit a form. It is to maintain a repeatable reporting process that management can understand and support.

      FINRA member firms submit FOCUS reports through eFOCUS under applicable SEC financial reporting requirements. The specific filing, frequency, and schedules depend on the firm's activities and regulatory profile.
      [FINRA provides current eFOCUS filing information, forms, and technical guidance](https://www.finra.org/filing-reporting/efocus).

      text: Net Capital Monitoring Before a Deadline Becomes a Problem
      color: text-dark
      type: TitleBlock
    text: |
      Net capital compliance should be monitored from current records, not reconstructed only when a filing is due. Regular calculations help management understand excess net capital, early-warning thresholds, liquidity, non-allowable assets, expense commitments, and the effect of planned transactions or withdrawals.

      An effective process establishes who updates the books, when the computation is prepared, what supporting schedules are required, who reviews the result, and how a potential deficiency is escalated. The cadence should reflect the firm's business model, minimum requirement, transaction activity, and financial condition.
