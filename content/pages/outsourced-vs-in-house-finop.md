    title:
      text: What an Outsourced FINOP Model Looks Like
      color: text-dark
      type: TitleBlock
    text: |
      An outsourced FINOP provides registered financial and operations principal support through an external engagement. A fractional FINOP is a version of this model in which the time commitment and operating cadence are matched to the firm's recurring needs.

      A well-structured engagement is not limited to submitting a filing. It establishes who maintains the underlying records, what the FINOP reviews, when information is delivered, how net capital is monitored, who owns each regulatory deadline, and how issues are escalated.

      For a smaller introducing broker-dealer, the cadence may include a documented monthly close and review, periodic net capital monitoring, FOCUS preparation, annual-audit coordination, and additional support around examinations, material changes, or capital events.

    title:
      text: When an In-House FINOP May Fit Better
      color: text-dark
      type: TitleBlock
    text: |
      A full-time internal FINOP may make sense when the firm has enough daily financial and operational activity to require continuous coverage. That can include a larger accounting organization, more complex business lines, custody or clearing responsibilities, significant transaction volume, frequent capital decisions, or extensive supervisory responsibilities.

      An internal model can provide immediate access and close integration with management, accounting, operations, and compliance. It also creates a fixed staffing commitment and requires the firm to recruit, retain, and support someone with the correct qualifications and experience.

      The important question is whether the role contains full-time work that genuinely benefits from permanent internal capacity. A title alone does not create the controls, records, reconciliations, and review routines that the FINOP needs.

    title:
      text: Five Factors to Compare
      color: text-dark
      type: TitleBlock
    text: |
      **1. Firm scope and complexity**  
      Consider business lines, clearing structure, minimum net capital, transaction volume, customer-account responsibilities, and open regulatory matters.

      **2. Required operating cadence**  
      Determine whether the firm needs continuous daily involvement or a disciplined recurring schedule with additional coverage around filings, audits, and examinations.

      **3. Internal accounting support**  
      Clarify who handles bookkeeping, reconciliations, bill payment, payroll, revenue schedules, and document collection. The FINOP needs reliable inputs regardless of the delivery model.

      **4. Continuity and responsiveness**  
      Define backup coverage, response expectations, information access, and how urgent capital or regulatory issues will be handled.

      **5. Total operating cost**  
      Compare the complete cost of recruiting and maintaining an internal position with the actual scope of an external engagement. The lowest quoted fee is not necessarily the lowest-risk model.

      FINRA assigns the designated FINOP direct responsibility for financial reports, supporting books and records, financial-responsibility rules, and back-office financial operations. The chosen model must give the principal enough access and authority to perform those duties. [FINRA Rule 1220 describes those responsibilities](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/1220).

  - type: GenericSection
    title:
      text: Questions to Ask a Prospective Outsourced FINOP
      color: text-dark
      type: TitleBlock
    text: |
      Before selecting an outsourced or fractional FINOP, ask:

      - Which broker-dealer models and registration categories do you support?
      - Which FINRA qualifications and registrations do you hold?
      - What is included in the recurring monthly scope?
      - Who prepares the underlying books, reconciliations, and schedules?
      - How often will net capital be computed or reviewed?
      - Who prepares, reviews, and files each FOCUS report?
      - How do you support the annual audit and regulatory examinations?
      - What information-access and review deadlines will the firm need to meet?
      - How are urgent issues and potential capital concerns escalated?
      - How are workpapers, approvals, and review evidence documented?

      Specific answers reveal whether the relationship is an operating model or merely a list of general services.

    items:
      - question: Is an outsourced FINOP the same as a fractional FINOP?
        answer: >-
          The terms overlap. Outsourced describes an external delivery model,
          while fractional emphasizes that the ongoing time commitment is
          right-sized rather than full-time. A professional may provide both an
          outsourced and fractional FINOP engagement.
      - question: Does outsourcing transfer the broker-dealer's regulatory responsibility?
        answer: >-
          No. The engagement can provide registered principal expertise and
          defined oversight, but the broker-dealer and its management retain
          their applicable responsibilities. Clear information flow, access,
          documentation, and escalation remain essential.
      - question: Can a firm move from outsourced to in-house coverage later?
        answer: >-
          Yes. A documented outsourced operating model can help establish the
          records, calendars, workpapers, and controls needed for an orderly
          transition when the firm's scale eventually supports a full-time
          internal position.
